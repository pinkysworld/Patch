\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related-work}

Patch sits at the intersection of first-class state change, effect systems, quantitative typing, capabilities and permissions, Hoare/refinement verification, explicit program changes, reversible programming, event-sourced histories, provenance, translation validation, and proof-carrying evidence. The relevant distinction is architectural rather than an expressiveness ordering: several of the systems below can state properties richer than Patch's current contract language. Patch asks what becomes available when the language's modeled application-state mutation route itself carries a small semantic delta vocabulary that is reused for execution, authority, history, provenance, and scoped assurance.

\paragraph{First-class and reified state change.}
Plaid makes state change first class and models objects as changing abstract states \cite{sunshine2011plaid}; Worlds reifies program state to control the scope of side effects \cite{warth2011worlds}. These establish that first-class state transition and reified state are not new ideas. Patch's question is narrower: ordinary modeled application-state updates pass through a semantic delta representation whose operation and, for a supported numeric fragment, magnitude also feed the authority vocabulary and recorded history.

\paragraph{Effect systems and effect operations.}
Classical polymorphic effect systems conservatively approximate side effects \cite{lucassen1988effects}. Algebraic accounts identify effect annotations with operation symbols \cite{kammar2012algebraic}; parametric effect monads \cite{katsumata2014parametric} and effect quantales \cite{gordon2017flow} provide compositional and order-sensitive foundations. Recent temporal-effect analysis infers context-sensitive dependent summaries over event traces and can express event summation, resource use, cost, and equal event magnitude \cite{nicola2025temporal}. This literature supplies semantic operations, interprocedural summaries, trace sensitivity, and quantitative reasoning. Patch investigates a more restricted coupling: the operation vocabulary is derived from the modeled state-update form and then reused by the contract and tooling layers.

\paragraph{Effects and capabilities.}
Effects-as-Capabilities connects effect requirements to capabilities \cite{brachthaeuser2020effects}, and System C further reconciles capability- and effect-based reasoning \cite{brachthaeuser2022boxes}. Patch therefore does not rely on the combination of effects and capabilities as its distinction. Its contract mechanism instead asks whether authority over target, operation, and bounded magnitude can be obtained from the same semantic lineage as the modeled mutation itself. In this paper, a \emph{Semantic Change Contract} means the inferred semantic Change Signature together with the declared Change Capability policy against which that signature is checked.

\paragraph{Quantitative and dependent effects.}
Graded modal types support quantitative reasoning about program resources and effects \cite{orchard2019quantitative}. Dependent-effect semantics permit grades that depend on program values \cite{kura2026dependent}, and dependent temporal effects provide value- and history-sensitive inference \cite{nicola2025temporal}. Patch's bounded magnitude is consequently not a claim of first quantitative or value-dependent effects. It is a deliberately small state-delta quantity attached to a target/operation pair, chosen so the same vocabulary can be reflected in mutation records, policies, diagnostics, and the mechanized bridge introduced in Section~\ref{sec:formal-assurance}.

\paragraph{Permissions, ownership, typestate, and state-sensitive effects.}
Borrowing permissions reason statically about aliases and access rights \cite{naden2012borrowing}; Mezzo uses affine and duplicable permissions for ownership, aliasing, and typestate changes \cite{pottier2013mezzo}. Revocable capabilities make capability availability flow-sensitive and can encode typestate transitions \cite{jia2026revocable}. InvalML tracks permanent and temporary invalidation of sensitive values with a polymorphic type-and-effect system \cite{gao2025invalidation}. These systems primarily organize authority around aliases, ownership, invalidation, protocols, or capability lifetime. Patch instead organizes its current authority fragment around the semantic delta permitted at a modeled application-state target.

\paragraph{Hoare, dependent, and refinement-style verification.}
Hoare Type Theory integrates pre/post specifications with mutable higher-order state \cite{nanevski2008htt}. F* supports effectful verification through dependent types and predicate-transformer semantics \cite{swamy2016fstar}; Dijkstra monads derive weakest-precondition reasoning for user-defined effects \cite{ahman2017dijkstra}. Such systems can express state relations at least as precise as Patch's bounded updates. Patch trades that generality for a restricted automatically derived vocabulary. The question evaluated here is whether this smaller vocabulary can be carried from modeled mutation to policy and tooling without introducing a second general-purpose specification for the evaluated cases.

\paragraph{Explicit changes, edits, and patch theory.}
COPE advocates change-oriented programming environments \cite{dig2016cope}; Edit Transactions group live program edits into dynamically scoped change sets \cite{mattis2017edittransactions}. Higher-order change calculi support incremental computation \cite{cai2014changes}, while edit lenses \cite{hofmann2012edit} and homotopical patch theory \cite{anguilhomotopical2016} provide rich theories of edits and repository patches. Their primary changed entities and goals differ from Patch's runtime application-state commit route. Patch uses explicit change at the application-state level and asks how far that representation can support semantic authority and assurance.

\paragraph{Reversible programming and event-sourced histories.}
Reversible languages such as Janus provide backward as well as forward execution and derive inverse behavior from reversible program structure \cite{yokoyama2007janus,schordan2020reversible}. Patch does not require reversible control flow. Its undo facilities reuse recorded semantic Changes; a \texttt{set} may require a recorded before-value rather than being intrinsically invertible. Event sourcing treats state-changing operations as authoritative history from which application state can be reconstructed and has been studied both as an evolution challenge and an observability-oriented architecture \cite{overeem2017eventsourcing,alongi2022eventsourced}. That is close architectural prior art for durable, inspectable state transitions. Patch moves the idea into the language's modeled application-state mutation route and couples it to a small operation/magnitude authority vocabulary and formal evidence.

\paragraph{Assurance and provenance.}
Translation validation \cite{necula2000translation} and Proof-Carrying Code \cite{necula1997pcc} establish patterns for checking evidence produced by unverified components. Patch's generated Lean certificates and independent validators follow that assurance pattern. Whyline-style debugging provides rich causal/provenance querying prior art \cite{ko2008whyline}; Patch's \texttt{why} and history facilities are tooling consequences of recorded semantic deltas, not a new theory of causal debugging.

\paragraph{Positioning summary.}
Two axes are central to the present paper. First, Patch makes a structured semantic Change the sole modeled route for post-creation application-state mutation in the formal core and source discipline. Second, its current contract vocabulary carries target, operation, and bounded numeric magnitude derived along that mutation lineage. Prior work provides stronger mechanisms along many individual dimensions; the paper does not rank those systems by expressiveness. The contribution tested here is the coupling itself: the new Lean bridge relates a well-formed committed Change to a contract-level Effect and proves that, for the supported singleton numeric fragment, an allowed bounded effect constrains the actual before/after magnitude. The implementation and artifact then reuse corresponding semantic information for signatures, policies, history, undo/provenance, and selected direct-WebAssembly assurance paths.

The scope is explicit. The formal bridge currently excludes fields, multi-operation Changes, collection/text operations, \texttt{clear}, and sign-indeterminate source ranges. Foreign code, external I/O, GUI-toolkit internal state, and unsupported language fragments are not silently promoted into the modeled mutation theorem. These boundaries motivate the external-validity and cost questions discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} rather than an expressiveness claim against the systems above.
